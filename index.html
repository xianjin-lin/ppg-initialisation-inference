Wrist-PPG heart-rate estimation · paired evaluation

Does a better starting point still help once the inference rule changes?

Initialisation is chosen when a network is trained. Causal recursion is applied afterwards, to the frozen network’s outputs. This page lets you inspect how the two choices were crossed on identical windows, and what the stored results do and do not show.

1 · Paired design

Two choices, made at two different stages

The same network and target-training recipe start from either B (random initialisation) or U (clinical-source initialisation). Every frozen B and U model is then read out twice on the same windows: directly, and through a fixed causal recursion.

  1. Training stage

    Initialisation

    B random weights, or U weights from supervised heart-rate training on clinical PPG (MIMIC-III-Ext-PPG; no PPG-DaLiA data).

    Then the same target recipe on PPG-DaLiA: 15 participant-separated folds × 3 paired repetitions.

  2. Frozen

    No parameter is retrained or searched after this point.

  3. Inference stage

    Readout

    Direct: each 10-s window on its own. Recursive: a fixed causal update over contiguous windows, adapted from BeliefPPG, with a transition matrix fitted on the fold’s training participants and shared by B and U.

Endpoint definitions and what is held fixed
MAE
Mean absolute error, bpm.
F5
Percentage of windows with absolute error above 5 bpm. Absolute values in %; differences in percentage points (pp).
E20
Mean positive excess above 20 bpm, i.e. mean of max(0, error − 20), bpm.
C20
Contribution of errors above 20 bpm to the overall MAE, i.e. mean of error × 1(error > 20) over all windows, bpm. It is not the mean of severe windows, a percentage or a share.

The 5- and 20-bpm thresholds are analysis definitions, not clinical safety criteria. Within each setting, all comparisons share windows, references and participant weighting. Repetitions are averaged within participant first; participants are then weighted equally. Validation-selected learning-rate variants are a separate analysis and are not part of this 2 × 2.

Reading the interaction

The interaction asks whether recursion changes the endpoint by a different amount under U than under B:

(Urec − Udirect) − (Brec − Bdirect)

    How to read the intervals and signs

    Intervals are descriptive 95% participant-bootstrap percentile intervals (10,000 paired resamples), conditional on the evaluated fits. They are not significance tests. An interval that includes zero is not evidence of no effect, equivalence or independence.

    Signs: for U − B, negative values mean lower values under U. For recursive − direct, negative values mean lower values under recursion. For the interaction, positive values mean the recursive-minus-direct change was more positive under U than under B (a smaller reduction or a larger increase); it does not by itself say that either effect is an improvement.

    The interaction interval is its own stored paired interval. It is never formed from the two separate intervals. No test of whether effects differ between settings or datasets was made.

    Display values are rounded to three decimals with explicit signs; the data file keeps the stored full-precision values.

    What one row represents

    Each row is one evaluated person in that dataset, in fixed identifier order. On DaLiA, a row averages the three paired repetitions of the participant’s own held-out fold. On WESAD, which was never used for fitting, each participant is scored by all 15 DaLiA fold models; a row averages the 45 paired fold-model × repetition differences. Repetitions and fold models are fits of the same person, not additional people.

    DaLiA identifiers are S1–S15; WESAD identifiers are S2–S11 and S13–S17 and follow WESAD numbering. Equal labels do not identify the same person across datasets, and participant overlap between the datasets is unverified. Highlighting links B and U rows within one dataset only.

    Counts: a participant counts as an increase if the stored difference is above +1×10−12, a decrease if below −1×10−12; anything in between is reported as a tie and never as an increase.

    4 · Severe-error duration Real frozen results Exploratory

    Less isolated burden, more sustained burden on average

    C20 split into the contributions of isolated (1–3), intermediate (4–14) and sustained (≥15) runs of severe windows (error > 20 bpm, 2-s window starts). Bars are stored participant-equal means in bpm; each stack sums to that readout’s C20.

    Evidence notes for the duration decomposition

    Separately classified. Runs were classified separately for direct and recursive readout, so a window can change class or stop being severe. The bars describe how C20 is redistributed among duration classes; they do not track individual errors from one class into another, and they cannot separate prolonged, merged, reclassified or newly created errors.

    Descriptive co-occurrence. In every setting, recursion lowered overall C20 while the average sustained contribution rose. This is a description, not a confirmed mechanism.

    Prespecified rule not met. The frozen summary rule required, for both initialisations, a higher isolated than sustained repair rate in at least 12 of 15 participants. Sustained runs under direct readout occurred in too few participants (rates defined for 4–11), so the rule was not met in any setting, although the isolated-contribution and sensitivity conditions held.

    Plan status. HRNet analyses followed a frozen decomposition plan; TEMPONet-191 applications were additional analyses. All are exploratory.

    5 · Recursion lab Synthetic

    How a causal recursion carries history forward

    A toy sequence of 60 windows (2-s stride) generated in your browser. At each window a toy “network output” p[t] over 191 one-bpm bins (30–220 bpm) is combined with the previous state through a toy transition prior:

    s[t] = normalise((T × s[t−1]) ⊙ p[t])

    Toy controls (not paper hyperparameters)

    These sliders change only the toy example. They are not sensitivity analyses and say nothing about the trained models. Changing a slider recomputes the whole 60-window toy run and keeps the current window; changing the preset returns to window 0 and pauses.

    What this lab is, and is not

    All values are generated deterministically in the browser from fixed toy seeds. The transition matrix uses the same Gaussian-in-log-heart-rate construction and half-bin edges as described in the manuscript, but with a toy spread; it is not one of the trained DaLiA fold matrices. Readouts use the local ±5-bin rule around the most probable bin, with offsets beyond 30 or 220 bpm clamped to the boundary bin (which then enters the average more than once). The state is reset to uniform at window 0 and at any gap.

    The lab shows the arithmetic of the update. It does not reproduce any participant, any reported effect size, real-time behaviour or a clinical mechanism. The manuscript did not establish lag, smoothing or persistence as the cause of the observed individual increases.

    The animation never starts by itself, stops at the last window, and pauses when this section leaves the screen or the tab is hidden.

    The recursion lab, generated in the browser from toy seeds. Not participant data, not a trained model, not a validation result.
